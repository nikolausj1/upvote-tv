After 10 years as a software engineer, here's what I wish someone told me on day one

I've been in the industry for a decade now, and looking back there are things I wish I'd internalized much earlier in my career. I'm not talking about technical skills - those you pick up naturally. I'm talking about the meta-skills that nobody teaches you.


First, your ability to communicate clearly is worth more than your ability to write clever code. I've seen brilliant engineers stall their careers because they couldn't explain their work to non-technical stakeholders.


Second, learn to say "I don't know" early and often. The instinct to pretend you understand something when you don't will cost you weeks of wasted effort. Nobody expects you to know everything.
